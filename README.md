## What "unlimited traffic" actually limits

Every plan is labelled "unmetered port (fair-share)", and the equivalent monthly volume ranges from 4+ TB on the 10 Mbps Promo up to 309+ TB on Procyon's 1 Gbps port. That number describes what the port could theoretically move if it ran flat out for a month. It is not a quota, and JustHost doesn't bill overages. The site's own line is "you pay only for disk space."

The constraint is the port itself. Those 300 Mbps, 500 Mbps and 750 Mbps figures are shared capacity on a fair-share basis, which means your actual throughput depends on what other customers on the same host are doing at that moment. Nothing here is a guaranteed rate.

How much is guaranteed bandwidth? **$333.33/month for a guaranteed 1 Gbps.** For scale: that is 68 times the monthly price of Sirius and about twice the price of Procyon, the most expensive plan on the list. Practically nobody buying a $5 VPS needs it, but the number is worth knowing, because it tells you exactly what the advertised 300 Mbps to 1 Gbps figures are and aren't.

If the port turns out to be your bottleneck, JustHost sells bandwidth in increments: **$2.50/month per additional 10 Mbit**. Going from 300 Mbps to 400 Mbps costs $2.50/month, and it stays fair-share.

## Which tier do you actually need?

**512 MB / 5 GB — VPS Promo at $1.99.** A 10 Mbps port works out to roughly 1.25 MB/s peak. That covers a static site, a monitoring node, a small proxy, a DNS resolver or a jump host. It does not comfortably cover a modern control panel plus MySQL plus a PHP stack; those want 1 GB before anything else. The equivalent monthly traffic figure of 4+ TB sounds generous until you notice a single large backup restore would take hours at that port speed.

**1 GB — New Star at $3.81 or Sirius at $4.85.** This is the real decision point on the whole price list. New Star gives you 10 GB of disk and a 100 Mbps port. Sirius gives you double the disk and triple the port for $1.04 more per month. Unless $1.04 is decisive, Sirius is the better buy: 10 GB fills up fast once you add a database and a couple of container images.

**2 GB — Arcturus at $6.92 or Double Star at $7.08.** Double Star has two cores, but only 20 GB of disk and a 200 Mbps port. Arcturus has one core, 30 GB and 300 Mbps, and costs 16 cents less. Take Double Star only if you have a workload that genuinely parallelises across cores. Most single-application deployments don't.

**2 cores / 2 GB — Alpha Centauri at $8.99.** 40 GB and a 400 Mbps port. This is where a small multi-container setup starts to fit without constant disk anxiety.

**4 GB — Vega at $13.44.** 2 cores, 80 GB, 500 Mbps. The disk jump from 40 GB to 80 GB is the main reason to move up here.

**4 cores / 4 GB — Capella at $18.30 versus Bright Star at $9.80.** Same cores and RAM, but Capella has four times the disk (120 GB versus 30 GB) and a 750 Mbps port against 300 Mbps, for $8.50 more. If you're storing media, build artifacts or database backups, the $8.50 buys a lot. If you just need CPU, Bright Star is the value pick on the entire list at $9.80 for 4 cores.

**8 GB and above — Rigel at $27.94.** 4 cores, 200 GB, 750 Mbps. Everything above Rigel scales disk and cores but holds the port at 750 Mbps until Procyon's 1 Gbps. Antares ($33.12), Betelgeuse ($45.54), Spica ($61.06), Altair ($81.76) and Aldebaran ($123.16) all sit on the same 750 Mbps port with the same 232+ TB traffic equivalent; you're paying purely for cores, RAM and disk. If none of those three is your bottleneck, stay on Rigel.

## The Hong Kong question, and what a third-party test found

JustHost's English price pages don't list which data centres you can pick, so nothing here can be confirmed as a current location from the official site alone. What exists is a third-party review published on 11 April 2026 that tested a Hong Kong instance. Treat everything in this section as that review's findings from that date, not as a current official specification.

That review describes KVM virtualisation, NVMe SSD arrays, 300 Mbps to 1 Gbps ports, one IPv4 plus one IPv6 per server, and a control panel with one-click data centre switching and one-click IP replacement. The CPU was an E5-2690 v4 running with BBR, on JustHost's own network, which the review calls "baxet."

Measured numbers from that test: disk I/O at 460 MB/s, FIO 4K read/write at 78 MB/s with roughly 19.6K IOPS, GeekBench 6 at 592 single-core and 1,736 quad-core. The reviewer's conclusion was "performance is average, adequate for regular tasks," which lines up with the hardware generation.

Routing is the part that decides whether this works for you. All three Chinese networks routed outbound traffic through the US. On the return path, China Telecom and China Unicom also routed through the US, while China Mobile performed acceptably toward Shanghai and Guangzhou but routed through the US toward Beijing. The reviewer's recommendation was to pair the server with a relay if speed matters.

Streaming and AI service checks from the same test: US Gemini and Steam Currency worked, Singapore TikTok worked, Hong Kong Disney+ and Netflix worked. **Claude and ChatGPT did not unlock.** The IP is registered as broadcast.

A separate summer 2026 write-up describes the network as pure international routing with no mainland China optimisation, and puts the data centre count at roughly 50 with up to 50 free IP changes. Both are consistent with the April findings on routing.

## Where the promo codes stand

JustHost runs its discounts on a calendar, and the codes change every time.

The May 2026 campaign, published on the official blog on 4 May, offered MAY50 for up to 50% off new VPS orders and MAY40 for up to 40% off renewals, valid until 31 May, limited to selected locations and to the New Star, Double Star, Bright Star, Sirius, Arcturus, Alpha Centauri, Vega, Capella and Rigel plans. That window closed months ago. The terms attached to it are still the pattern to expect: one code per order, no stacking with other discounts, and the code only works on the named plans and named data centres.

The official blog announced a 21st-birthday promotion on 21 October 2026 with two codes, one for 40% off a new VPS and one for 21% off. A third-party deals site, writing on 29 October, listed a Double 11 set running through 14 November: 11VPS50 for 50% off the first billing cycle, 11VPS40 for 40% off on orders of six months or more with the same 40% carried into renewals, and 11VPS35 for 35% off. Those codes were not visible on JustHost's plan pages when this was written, so confirm the amount on the order page before you pay. If a code and the standing 20% annual discount don't stack, the annual discount is usually the larger and more reliable of the two on cheaper plans.

## Add-ons and payment methods

Beyond the extra IPv4 at $3.31/month and bandwidth at $2.50/month per 10 Mbit, JustHost sells systems administration at **$9.16/hour**. That's an hourly rate, not a managed plan, so budget accordingly if you want someone else to run the box.

Every plan includes a free IPv6 address, unlimited traffic, SSH access and DDoS protection, and the price page also lists free migration and a choice of storage type. Payment options are cryptocurrencies, credit cards, PayPal, Alipay and UnionPay.

No refund policy, free trial or uptime guarantee appears in any of the material reviewed for this article, so don't assume one exists. If a cancellation window matters to your decision, ask support before you pay rather than after.

## Choosing, in one pass

- A static site, a proxy or a monitoring node: **VPS Promo, $1.99/month.**
- A panel, a small app or a handful of containers: **Sirius, $4.85/month or $3.88/month annually.** This is the plan most readers should start on.
- Parallel workloads on a tight budget: **Bright Star, $9.80/month** for 4 cores, accepting 30 GB of disk and a 300 Mbps port.
- Storage-heavy workloads: **Capella, $18.30/month** for 120 GB at 750 Mbps.
- Memory-heavy, storage-light: **Rigel, $27.94/month** for 8 GB and 200 GB.
- IPv6-only infrastructure with no IPv4 clients: **the IPv6 line**, roughly 26% cheaper at the low end. Add the $3.31/month IPv4 only if you actually need it, and check the maths first, because it usually erases the saving.

Two things to confirm at checkout before you enter card details. First, the actual price on the order page, since the standard list and the Special offers page disagree on several plans.
